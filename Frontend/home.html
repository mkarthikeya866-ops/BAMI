The Discovery Challenge

Why Discovering a Medicine Takes So Long

Developing a new therapeutic drug is one of the most complex scientific endeavors in the world. Before a treatment ever reaches patients, researchers must find a molecular starting point.

The Needle in a Chemical Haystack

Traditional wet-lab synthesis and cellular assays take weeks per compound. Examining even 10,000 candidates can cost millions of dollars and require years of bench work.

This is where computational screening helps: rather than testing molecules at random, machine-learning models estimate which molecules are most likely to interact with the target, allowing researchers to prioritize top candidates for lab validation.

Practical Glossary

Drug Discovery & Bioactivity Dictionary

A plain-language reference for researchers, students, and curious minds. Every term is defined simply, with clear illustrations and zero assumed background in chemistry or machine learning.

Dictionary • Term 01

Biological Target

🔒 Analogy: A cellular lock or light switch

Figure 1: A biological target protein acts like a cellular lock. Candidate molecules are screened to estimate whether they fit into the active binding pocket.

A biological target is a specific protein, enzyme, or receptor in the body that researchers want to affect to modify a disease process. When an enzyme is overactive—like enzymes promoting tumor growth—blocking that target can slow down or treat the illness.

Our Project Focus (EGFR): BAMI specifically targets EGFR (Epidermal Growth Factor Receptor, UniProt P00533), a cell-surface protein that signals cells to divide. In non-small cell lung cancer, EGFR is often hyperactive; blocking its pocket halts tumor signals. BAMI also supports COX-2, the enzyme producing pain and inflammation.
Dictionary • Term 02

Molecule

⚛️ Analogy: A key sculpted from individual atoms

Figure 2: A molecule is a group of atoms joined in a particular arrangement. Its three-dimensional shape determines whether it can bind to target proteins.

A molecule is a distinct group of two or more atoms joined together in a specific, stable spatial arrangement by chemical bonds. Small molecules (like aspirin, ibuprofen, or gefitinib) have precise 3D architectures that allow them to slip into cellular binding pockets.

How BAMI represents it: Computers represent molecules using text strings called SMILES. BAMI converts SMILES into 2,048-bit numerical circular fingerprints (Morgan fingerprints) to feed into machine learning.
Dictionary • Term 03

Drug

💊 Analogy: A published, certified textbook vs. an early rough draft

Figure 3: Machine learning identifies early candidate leads. Every lead must undergo rigorous wet-lab testing and human clinical trials before becoming a medicine.

A drug is an officially approved medicine tested extensively in laboratories, animal models, and three phases of human clinical trials to prove that it is both safe and effective for preventing, managing, or treating a medical condition.

Critical Reality: A molecule predicted active by BAMI is not automatically a drug. It is merely a computational starting lead. It has not been tested in living systems for metabolism, liver toxicity, blood-brain barrier transport, or therapeutic dosing.
Dictionary • Term 04

Bioactive Molecule

⚡ Analogy: A key that physically turns a cellular tumbler

Figure 4: Bioactive compounds cause measurable biological responses. They can be helpful, neutral, or harmful; bioactivity alone is not proof of safety.

A bioactive molecule is a molecule that interacts with a biological system and causes a measurable effect. It binds to a cellular target and modulates its behavior—for example, shutting down a kinase enzyme or triggering receptor signaling.

Essential Distinction: Bioactivity does not mean the molecule is safe or beneficial. Snake venom, bacterial toxins, and lethal poisons are bioactive. “Bioactive” simply means a biological interaction occurs.
Dictionary • Term 05

Bioactivity

📊 Analogy: The volume of sound produced when an instrument is struck

Bioactivity is the measurable effect or response that a molecule produces on a biological target, cell, or organism. In pharmaceutical science, it is quantified using standard laboratory metrics such as the half-maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50).

How this project defines it: In BAMI, bioactivity is treated as a binary classification: Active (compounds that bind strongly, typically with IC50 ≤ 10 μM or pIC50 ≥ 6.0) versus Inactive (compounds that fail to bind effectively).
Dictionary • Term 06

Structural Similarity

🧩 Analogy: Two keys with matching teeth and grooves

Figure 5: Molecules with similar core skeletons (like salicylic acid and aspirin) often share activity, but minor changes can alter effect.

Structural similarity measures how alike two molecules are in their chemical structures, atomic layouts, and functional groups. According to the Similar Property Principle, similar molecules frequently exhibit similar biological properties.

The "Activity Cliff" Caveat: Similarity is not proof. Changing just a single atom or bond angle can completely eliminate binding activity or introduce severe toxicity. BAMI computes mathematical fingerprints to detect these subtle differences.
Dictionary • Term 07

Prediction / Model Score

🎯 Analogy: A calibrated weather forecast probability

In BAMI, a prediction is the computational classification generated by our trained machine-learning model, accompanied by specific numerical scores:

  • • Predicted Class: Binary result indicating whether the molecule is classified as Active or Inactive against the target.
  • • Calibrated Probability (EGFR): For EGFR, the raw model score is calibrated using Platt/isotonic scaling into an empirical probability percentage, evaluated against an optimal threshold (e.g., 44.0%).
  • • Model Score (COX-2): For COX-2, the system reports the model probability score evaluated against a 50.0% classification decision threshold.
  • • Molecular Descriptors: Calculated physical properties (Molecular Weight, LogP, TPSA, HBD, HBA, Rotatable Bonds) assessing Lipinski Rule-of-Five drug-likeness.
Scientific Meaning: These scores are computational estimates, never guarantees of clinical or laboratory outcome.
Dictionary • Term 08

XGBoost

🌳 Analogy: A committee of hundreds of specialized decision trees

XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting) is an industry-standard machine-learning algorithm. Rather than relying on simple linear formulas or hard-coded rules, it builds an ensemble of sequential decision trees where each new tree specifically learns from and corrects the mistakes made by previous trees.

Why BAMI uses it: XGBoost excels at analyzing sparse, high-dimensional chemical data. It scans all 2,048 bits of a molecule's Morgan circular fingerprint, discovering subtle nonlinear patterns that separate active binders from inert compounds with high computational efficiency.
Dictionary • Term 09 • Preclinical Safety

ADMET (Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion & Toxicity)

🛡️ Analogy: A racing car needs safe brakes, steering, and fuel economy, not just top speed

Figure 9: The five critical pharmacokinetic and safety pillars: Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, and Toxicity.

ADMET stands for Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion, and Toxicity. A candidate molecule may be extremely active against EGFR or COX-2 in a test tube, yet fail completely as a therapeutic drug if it cannot dissolve, cross the gut, or causes toxic damage to organs like the liver or heart.

A Absorption Aqueous solubility (logS), Caco-2 cell permeability, and human intestinal absorption (HIA).
D Distribution Blood-brain barrier permeability (BBBP) and plasma protein binding fraction unbound (fu).
M Metabolism CYP450 enzyme inhibition (CYP3A4, CYP2D6, CYP2C9) to prevent polypharmacy interactions.
E Excretion Quantitative estimate of drug-likeness (QED) and renal vs. hepatic elimination pathway drivers.
T Toxicity Ames bacterial mutagenicity, FDA DILIst liver injury, and cardiac hERG channel blockade risk.

The Technology Pipeline

How BAMI Screens Molecules Step-by-Step

Here is the exact computational pipeline executed under the hood when you enter a compound into BAMI.

Step 01

User Provides Molecule & Target

User Input UI

You choose a biological target (such as EGFR or COX-2) and provide a molecule by typing its common name (e.g., Aspirin, Gefitinib) or pasting its chemical SMILES notation.

Aspirin → COX-2
Step 02

Molecular Representation (Morgan Fingerprints)

RDKit Cheminformatics

Machine-learning models cannot read chemical drawings directly. RDKit calculates a circular Morgan fingerprint (radius 2, 2,048 bits) that encodes atom neighborhoods and bonding patterns into a binary vector of 0s and 1s.

[0, 1, 0, 0, 1 ... 2,048 bits]
Step 03

Trained XGBoost Model Estimates Activity

Gradient Boosted Trees

The 2,048 numerical features pass through our trained XGBoost decision trees. For EGFR, Platt/isotonic calibration maps the raw tree outputs into true probabilities evaluated against calibrated decision thresholds.

XGBoost → Score: 86.5%
Step 04

View Predictions & Available Scores

Real-Time Evaluation

The app displays the actual classification label (Active or Inactive), calibrated probability percentage or model score, decision threshold, and 6 Lipinski Rule-of-Five properties (MW, LogP, TPSA, HBD, HBA, Rotatable Bonds).

Scientific Integrity

Why Computation Matters — And Its Honest Limits

Computational screening is a powerful accelerator for early-stage discovery, but it is one tool in a multi-stage scientific pipeline.

✓ What This Tool Can Do

  • Fast Candidate Prioritization: Rank thousands of potential molecules rapidly against EGFR and COX-2, helping chemists choose which compounds to synthesize first.
  • Chemical Substructure Recognition: Detect subtle circular sub-structure patterns (2,048-bit Morgan fingerprints) statistically correlated with target binding.
  • Scaffold Extraction & SAR Exploration: Isolate Bemis-Murcko core rings and decompose peripheral substituents across structural series.
  • Comprehensive ADMET Profiling: Evaluate 10 endpoint-specific machine learning models for aqueous solubility, Caco-2 permeability, blood-brain barrier transport, CYP450 inhibition, and organ safety hazards (Ames, DILI, hERG).
  • Calibrated Confidence: Provide statistical confidence percentages and optimal decision thresholds rather than uncalibrated heuristic scores.

× What This Tool Cannot Do (Honest Limits)

  • Not Biological Proof: A high model score is a computational hypothesis, not physical proof of in vitro or in vivo biological efficacy.
  • Wet-Lab Assays Remain Mandatory: While our ADMET module identifies critical liabilities early, in vitro cellular assays and patch-clamp electrophysiology remain essential before clinical advancement.
  • No Whole-Body Physiological Simulation: Does not simulate patient-specific genetics, idiosyncratic immune reactions, or multi-organ systemic pharmacodynamics.
  • Research Triage Only, Never Medical Advice: Outputs are preclinical screening leads for laboratory scientists, never medical guidance or treatment advice for patients.
⚠️
Mandatory Wet-Lab Validation: All computational predictions must be validated through experimental wet-lab assays (enzymatic IC50 measurements, cell viability testing, and crystallographic binding studies) before any lead can advance.
