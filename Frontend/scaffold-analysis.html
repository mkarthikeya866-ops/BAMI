Scientific Distinction: Scaffold Matching vs. Molecular Similarity

Scaffold matching identifies 100% equivalence of the central ring-and-linker core. In contrast, Morgan Fingerprint Tanimoto similarity measures whole-molecule topological bit overlap, which includes peripheral substituents and functional groups. Two molecules can share an identical core yet have modest whole-molecule similarity if their substituents differ significantly in size, polarity, or branching.

Educational Primer

Understanding Bemis-Murcko Scaffold Analysis

How computational chemists partition molecular architectures into cores and substituents.

🏛️

What is a Bemis-Murcko Scaffold?

Introduced in 1996 by Guy Bemis and Mark Murcko, a scaffold is defined as the all-ring systems plus the connecting linkers between rings in a molecule. Terminal non-ring chains (such as methyl, hydroxyl, or halogen groups) are classified as peripheral substituents.

🔄

What is Scaffold Hopping?

Scaffold hopping involves replacing a molecule's core framework with a structurally distinct ring system while preserving key functional groups that interact with the biological target. It is used to overcome patent barriers, improve metabolic stability, or reduce off-target toxicity.

🎯

Structure-Activity Relationships (SAR)

Investigating a series of molecules with a shared scaffold allows researchers to observe how small peripheral substituent changes correlate with changes in biological potency or machine-learning model scores, identifying optimal R-group substitutions.

⚠️

Important Limitations

An identical scaffold does not guarantee identical biological activity. A single halogen substitution can create an activity cliff that eliminates binding. Predictions presented in BAMI are computational machine-learning estimates and must be confirmed with wet-lab experimental validation.
